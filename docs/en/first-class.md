# 🌅 First Session · Breath and Space: My Yoga in Brief

> This is what I talk about in the first class of the autumn course – and a map for the whole semester. Original text (Chinese): [Substack](https://supponoqui163292.substack.com/p/106). The matching practice is at the end, under "Practice for the first session".

## Introduction: Sharing, Not Testing

We practise together – it is not a test and not a performance. I hope that after class you still feel like coming back, even on tired days, rather than dreading it. Flexibility is not a prerequisite: we practise so that the breath flows more freely and the body gains more space.

---

## 1. Breath

**As long as you can breathe, you can practise yoga.** Practice starts with observing the breath – and that is also its goal, on the mat and beyond it.

- The depth and rhythm of your breath reflect your state right now. Anxiety, anger, distraction, shame, calm, joy … all of it is temporary; time, too, flows in the breath.
- **The pose is a means, not the goal.** In quiet sitting the mind wanders easily; dynamic stretching and balancing call for focus. When you drift off, try an unfamiliar, challenging movement – it brings your attention back.
- **The breath tells you whether a pose suits you.** If you hold your breath or can't breathe freely, take an easier variation and stay where it feels good.
- **The finger pointing at the moon**: cues and techniques are the "finger", awareness is the "moon". Don't cling to the finger and miss the moon. If you already know another yoga style, feel free to practise the way you are used to.

---

## 2. Space

What truly sets yoga practice apart is **space** – the capacity to respond to the world (joy and pain, pressure, provocation, change): tolerance, courage, decisiveness, empathy, perception and richness.

- Mastery is not measured by muscle mass, height, weight or flexibility, but by **acknowledging and accepting your own limits** – and widening them step by step from there.
- The visible pose is a good guide: first gather your focus, then gradually move towards subtler inner observation.
- **Gazing point (drishti)**: between the eyebrows, the distance, the mat, the ceiling … Direct your attention and the body follows by itself – like learning to snowboard: look where your fingers point, and the whole body coordinates.

Space grows from two things: **whole-body extension** and **a stable foundation**.

---

## 3. Whole-Body Extension

Stretching means lengthening the whole body – every part contributes a little length, instead of pulling at one spot.

- **Forward folds** (standing forward fold Uttanasana, seated forward fold, seated fold with one leg bent in): front and back body lengthen **at the same time** – not one side shortening while the other is pulled.
- **Backbends** (cobra, upward dog, tiger, wheel): the body is like an arched bridge – evenly loaded, lengthening at both ends.
- **Let the breath lead:**
  - **Inhale**: come back a little and lengthen first. Look into the distance or at your toes – there's no hurry to bring your face to your legs.
  - **Exhale**: let gravity take you slowly deeper.
  - Stay for a few breaths **close to your edge, but still breathing freely**.

---

## 4. A Stable Foundation

The foundation is the part of the body that touches the mat – different in sitting, tabletop, standing and hand support.

- **Sitting**: put a block under your sitting bones so your knees are lower than your hips and close to the floor. Stability relaxes the muscles and settles the mind. Seated postures: full lotus, half lotus, easy seat, full pigeon, thunderbolt (sitting on the heels).
- **Hands**: fingers slightly spread, index fingers forward, the base of the thumb and the heel of the hand press down; the inner arms engage and an outward rotation appears – like an octopus arm with suction cups on the floor.
- **Standing**: three points of the sole (heel, base of the big toe, base of the little toe) carry evenly, the inner legs are active; feet press down, hips lift, knees carry no pressure.
- **Tops of the feet on the floor**: in upward dog, crescent lunge and thunderbolt pose. It can feel strange at first – but it helps you get to know your body.
- **Feeling stability**:
  - **Organ support** (Donna Farhi): feel the digestive tract carrying the body's weight – not leaning forward or back, grounded like a yoga block. When the position is right, no extra muscle effort is needed to stay balanced.
  - **Balance with the gaze**: the leg doesn't need to go high – rising onto the toes or lifting a foot slightly is enough to practise stability.

---

## 5. Summary

**On the breath**
- **a. Observe the breath you already have**: breathing is instinctive, yet often overlooked. Together we count our breaths for one minute (one inhale + one exhale = one breath); then a few rounds of in through the nose, out through the mouth, before returning to nose breathing.
- **b. Breath and movement**: the inhale usually accompanies extending outward and upward; the exhale, grounding inward and downward.
- **c. The measure**: a smooth, natural breath – not the teacher's or anyone else's opinion – decides whether a pose suits you right now.

**On space**
Yoga is not about training single muscles or flexibility, but about expanding the space of the whole body and your ability to engage and control your muscles.
- **Downward dog**: try stepping forward between your hands in one step. Hands pressed firmly down, the back foot lands wherever it lands; as the back of the body opens, the foot gradually comes further forward.
- **Chaturanga**: feel the weight move forward, arms close to the sides, the whole body lowering as one. If it's hard, start with the knees down.

## Practice for the First Session

I don't tell it all in one go: about 10 minutes at the start on **sharing, not testing** and the **breath**; the rest flows into the poses as cues. Wheel, upward dog, full lotus and full pigeon are only mentioned as examples – in the first class we practise their entry-level versions.

| Time | Phase | Poses / exercise | What we feel |
|------|------|------------|-----------|
| 0–10′ | Arrive · talk | Introduction, breath | Sharing, not testing · as long as you can breathe … |
| 10–15′ | Observe the breath | Count breaths for 1 minute · a few rounds nose in, mouth out | Summary a |
| 15–25′ | Seated foundation | Easy seat (on a block, knees below hips) · seated side stretch · seated twist | Sitting bones as base · organ support · inhale extend, exhale ground |
| 25–35′ | Tabletop | Hand foundation · cat-cow · tiger · thunderbolt (tops of feet down) | Hands like suction cups · backbend as an arch · tops of the feet |
| 35–50′ | Standing | Mountain (three points of the sole) · standing forward fold (inhale half lift, gaze forward; exhale deepen with gravity) · downward dog (try stepping between the hands) · crescent lunge (back foot top down) · tree (gazing point) | Standing foundation · whole-body length · space · drishti |
| 50–58′ | Prone | Sphinx → cobra · chaturanga with knees down | Even backbend · lowering as one |
| 58–68′ | Cool-down | Head-to-knee (one leg bent in) · supine twist | Front and back lengthen together · stay where the breath is free |
| 68–75′ | Close | Final relaxation · chanting | Back to the breath |
