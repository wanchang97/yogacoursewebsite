# Who Breath · Ocean Breath

<p class="pose-sanskrit">Who Practice → Ujjayi Breath</p>

We breathe all the time — and the breath is the most direct way to get to know ourselves. This practice begins with gently blowing toward a candle flame and leads step by step into the **ocean breath** (Ujjayi) that runs through the whole vinyasa flow.

## 1 · Sit stable

Sit on the mat in easy pose, kneeling (vajrasana) or cross-legged. If the knees are higher than the hips, put a block under the buttocks — **a stable body supports a calm mind**. Spine upright, shoulders sink down and back, hands on the knees or the belly. Close the eyes gently, or rest the gaze on the floor in front of you.

## 2 · Feel the natural breath first

- **Widening sideways**: hands on belly and ribs — on the inhale feel belly and ribs expand outward, on the exhale draw in, the back lengthens softly.
- **Growing upward**: on the inhale feel the body grow taller, chest and belly space expanding upward with the air — the hand on the belly now feels a slight drawing in rather than pushing out. Exhale slowly through the nose; the body relaxes and settles.

## 3 · Who breath (candle breath)

<div class="br-box">
Inhale through the nose … exhale through the open mouth with a soft <b>“Who——”</b>, long and fine like wind.<br>
Imagine a burning candle in front of you — exhale <b>so gently that the flame doesn't go out</b>.<br>
Repeat a few times.
</div>

## 4 · Into ocean breath (Ujjayi)

<div class="br-box">
Next breath: inhale through the nose, start the exhale through the mouth … then <b>close the mouth and keep exhaling through the nose</b> — feel that the soft sound in the throat stays.<br>
Now breathe in and out only through the nose, keeping this gentle sound, <b>like the sound of the ocean</b>.
</div>

## 5 · Use the breath as your measure

- Not **too short or too loud** — if it gets short or loud, the pose is probably too demanding for your body today; take a step back.
- Not **too quiet** either — then you may no longer be fully with your breath.
- One inhale plus one exhale is **one breath**. Inhale — the body expands out and up; exhale — the body sinks down and toward the centre.
- Stay for 3–5 breaths in your own rhythm.

<div class="br-tip">
<b>Teaching tips</b><br>
· The first time, let everyone make an exaggerated “Who” and then soften it — students find the throat more easily.<br>
· The ocean sound only needs to be faintly audible to yourself and your neighbour, not to the whole room.<br>
· If anyone feels dizzy or uncomfortable, return to natural breathing.
</div>
