## 1 · Seated breathing & warm-up

<p class="note">Settle, breathe, then wake up the spine with side stretches, neck stretches and twists.</p>
<div class="flow-grid">
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/seated-pranayama.jpg" alt="Seated breathing" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>01</small>Seated breathing</div><div class="t">s1 4:57</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>A stable seat is the basis for breathing.</b></p><p>You can sit on a yoga block so that your knees are lower than your hips and rest close to the mat. A stable body brings relaxed muscles and a calm mind.</p><p>Common seated positions:</p><ul><li>🧘 <b>Full lotus</b>: each foot rests on the opposite thigh, close to the hip crease.</li><li><b>Half lotus</b>: one foot on the opposite thigh, the other leg on the mat.</li><li><b>Easy pose</b>: both legs rest on the mat.</li><li><b>Double pigeon</b> (fire log, Agnistambhasana): shins stacked, each foot above the opposite knee.</li></ul><p>Choose whatever lets you sit most stable and comfortable right now — once you are settled, begin to observe your breath.</p></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/seated-side-stretch.jpg" alt="Seated side stretch" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>02</small>Seated side stretch</div><div class="t">s1 5:18</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Inhale to grow tall, exhale to lengthen sideways.</b></p><p>Inhale, lift both arms in front of you and up. Exhale, one hand to the mat, the other arm over the ear, lengthen this side of the body. Stay 1–2 breaths, feel the side ribs open with the breath, then change sides.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>First grow long, then bend — the side bend is the result of a long spine, not of collapsing the waist.</li><li>Both sit bones stay grounded; the opposite hip does not lift.</li><li>The top arm is beside the ear, the shoulder sinks away from the ear.</li><li>Breathe into the lengthened side and feel the ribs expand and release.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/neck-stretch.jpg" alt="Neck stretch" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>03</small>Neck stretch</div><div class="t">s1 6:03</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Use the weight of the hand, not its strength.</b></p><p>Inhale, arms up. Exhale, one hand on the mat, the other hand over the head to the opposite ear, lengthen the side of the neck — 2 breaths. Then lower the gaze to stretch the back of the neck — 2 breaths. Change sides.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>The hand only rests on the head; let the weight of the arm do the work, don't pull.</li><li>Actively sink the shoulder on the stretched side — the further it moves from the ear, the deeper the stretch.</li><li>Looking down means turning the chin gently toward the armpit.</li><li>To come out, guide the head back to center with the hand before letting go.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>

Exhale, twist to one side: the opposite hand on the knee, the other hand behind the back resting at the waist. The upper body stays calm, the gaze slowly turns. 1–2 breaths.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Inhale to grow taller; exhale to turn a little further at that height.</li><li>The twist starts in the belly, then chest and shoulders, and only last the head and gaze.</li><li>Both sit bones stay evenly grounded.</li><li>The hand on the knee only assists — don't lever the body with the arm.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>

Exhale, twist to the other side, hand to the opposite knee, the other hand behind you at the waist, gaze slowly follows. 1–2 breaths.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Notice the difference between the sides — no need to make them equal, just observe.</li><li>Pause briefly at the end of each exhale and feel the belly draw in.</li><li>Come back slowly too: gaze first, then shoulders, chest, belly.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>

## 2 · Tabletop sequence

<p class="note">Build the foundation of the hands; cat–cow, thread the needle and tiger in motion and balance.</p>
<div class="flow-grid">
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/tabletop.jpg" alt="Tabletop" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>06</small>Tabletop</div><div class="t">s1 7:25</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>The hands become new "feet" — build the foundation first.</b></p><p>Hands under the shoulders, knees under the hips. Spread the fingers, press the finger roots and the base of the thumb into the floor, rotate the arms from inside to outside. Draw the navel back and up so the back becomes long and flat.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Index fingers point forward; spread the weight over the whole hand, especially the roots of index finger and thumb — don't sink into the wrists.</li><li>Outward rotation of the upper arms: the elbow creases turn slightly forward, the shoulders support the body fully.</li><li>Belly gently engaged, the back is a flat table top.</li><li>The neck continues the spine; gaze slightly in front of the hands.</li></ul><p>Easier: if the wrists complain, make fists or place a folded blanket under the heels of the hands.</p></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/hero-fold.jpg" alt="Extended child&#x27;s pose" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>07</small>Extended child&#x27;s pose</div><div class="t">s1 8:00</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>The start and end point of cat–cow.</b></p><p>From tabletop, sit back slowly toward the heels, hands stay in front, arms reach forward, forehead rests on the mat.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Hands press and arms lengthen while the hips sink back — the body is drawn apart from both ends.</li><li>Knees together or mat-wide so the belly can rest between the thighs.</li><li>If the hips don't reach the heels, place a blanket between thighs and calves.</li><li>Feel the breath in the back: on the inhale the back widens up and out.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><video src="figures/video/clips/cat-cow.mp4" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video><span class="tag clip">clip</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>08</small>Cat–cow flow</div><div class="t">s1 8:00–8:29</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>One breath, one movement.</b></p><p>Start in extended child's pose, hips on the heels. Inhale — gaze to the navel, cat, shift forward into tabletop. Exhale — lift the head, cow, back to child's pose. Repeat three times.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Let the breath move the body, not the other way round — the movement is as long as the breath.</li><li>In cat, round the spine vertebra by vertebra from the tailbone, navel draws in.</li><li>In cow, open the chest forward, shoulders away from the ears, don't crunch the neck.</li><li>Hands keep pressing; feel how the load on the wrists changes as you shift.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><video src="figures/video/clips/thread-needle-dyn.mp4" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video><span class="tag clip">clip</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>09</small>Thread the needle (dynamic)</div><div class="t">s1 8:33–9:09</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Inhale to open, exhale to thread through.</b></p><p>From tabletop, inhale, lift one arm and open this side of the chest. Exhale, thread the arm under the other one, shoulder down to the mat. Three times; the last time you can stay for three breaths.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>On the inhale the gaze follows the fingertips; the rotation comes from the thoracic spine, not the lower back.</li><li>On the exhale shoulder and cheek rest on the mat; hips stay above the knees.</li><li>The supporting hand keeps pressing to give the upper back room to rotate.</li><li>While holding, breathe into the space between the shoulder blades.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/thread-needle-balance.jpg" alt="Thread the needle · balance" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>10</small>Thread the needle · balance</div><div class="t">s1 9:22</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Balance challenge — step by step.</b></p><p>While holding the needle you can deepen gradually: ① top hand to the opposite waist; ② stretch the leg on the same side back; ③ lift the back leg off the floor and hold the balance.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Make each stage stable before moving on; if the breath gets short, go back one stage.</li><li>With the leg lifted, reaching long matters more than lifting high.</li><li>The shoulder on the mat doesn't carry everything — supporting hand and knee share the load.</li><li>Core engaged, pelvis level, don't roll open.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><video src="figures/video/clips/thread-needle-dyn-other.mp4" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video><span class="tag clip">clip</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>11</small>Thread the needle, other side</div><div class="t">s1 9:30–9:53</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Same rhythm, notice the difference.</b></p><p>Back to tabletop. Inhale, lift the other arm and open the chest; exhale, shoulder to the mat. Three times, stay three breaths the last time, then back to tabletop.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Inhale — look at the fingertips; exhale — shoulder down. Same rhythm as side one.</li><li>Notice tension in this shoulder blade and upper back; it need not match the other side.</li><li>Hips stay above the knees.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/thread-needle-balance-other.jpg" alt="Thread the needle · balance, other side" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>12</small>Thread the needle · balance, other side</div><div class="t">s1 10:00</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Balance challenge on the other side.</b></p><p>Deepen in the same way: ① top hand to the opposite waist → ② leg stretches back → ③ lift the back leg and balance.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Choose the stage your body can control today — control before height.</li><li>Keep the breath steady, don't hold it.</li><li>Release in reverse: leg down, hand back, then tabletop.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/leg-extend.jpg" alt="Extending one leg" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>13</small>Extending one leg</div><div class="t">s1 10:15</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Preparing for tiger: send the leg far back.</b></p><p>From tabletop, step one foot back, straighten this leg, toes on the floor, and gently rock forward and back.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>The back leg reaches from the hip, heel pushes back — feel the back of the leg.</li><li>Rocking forward and back moves the shoulders over the wrists and warms them up.</li><li>Pelvis stays level and facing down; the hip of the extended leg doesn't open.</li><li>This builds the hand–core–leg connection for tiger.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><video src="figures/video/clips/tiger-dyn.mp4" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video><span class="tag clip">clip</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>14</small>Tiger flow</div><div class="t">s1 10:15–10:34</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Inhale lift the leg, exhale knee to nose.</b></p><p>With the back leg extended, inhale and lift it. Exhale, bend the knee and draw it toward the nose, rounding the back. Three times.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Lift the leg only to the line of the body — don't sink into the lower back to lift higher.</li><li>On the exhale round the back like in cat, navel in; knee and nose meet in the middle.</li><li>Hands keep pressing, shoulders over the wrists, the body doesn't sway.</li><li>The slower you follow the breath, the more you feel the core working.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/tiger-balance.jpg" alt="Tiger · balance" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>15</small>Tiger · balance</div><div class="t">s1 10:52</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Tiger balance variations.</b></p><p>While holding you can deepen: ① extend the opposite arm and leg into one line; ② further: the opposite hand reaches back for the ankle — hold tiger steadily.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Stage one: one line from fingertips to toes, reaching in both directions.</li><li>When holding the ankle, kick the foot into the hand so the chest opens forward — don't pull the foot in.</li><li>Supporting hand and knee press firmly; keep the pelvis as level as possible.</li><li>If unsteady, stay in stage one — balance is also concentration practice.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/gate.jpg" alt="Gate pose" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>16</small>Gate pose</div><div class="t">s1 11:20</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Gate pose: open the groin, lengthen the side waist.</b></p><p>Place one foot out to the side, kneel upright and stretch the groin. Arms up, bend toward the extended leg three times, stretching the opposite waist; hold the last one for two breaths.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>The kneeling knee is under the hip; the extended foot is roughly in line with it.</li><li>Lengthen up first, then bend; the chest faces forward and doesn't collapse.</li><li>The pelvis stays steady — the stretch is in the upper side waist and between the ribs.</li><li>If the kneeling knee is uncomfortable, put a blanket under it.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>

## 3 · Mountain pose & sun salutation

<p class="note">From mountain pose, one full vinyasa: arms up → fold → plank → chaturanga → up-dog → down dog.</p>
<div class="flow-grid">
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/mountain.jpg" alt="Mountain pose" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>17</small>Mountain pose</div><div class="t">s1 14:01</div><div class="s"><a href="#/zh/poses/mountain">Pose card (中文) →</a><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>The starting point of every standing pose.</b></p><p>Place heel, the ball under the little toe and the ball under the big toe one after the other — build a stable stand. Activate the inner, outer and back of the legs. Pelvis level, spine upright, shoulders sink down and back, arms by your sides. Take three deep breaths.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Imagine three points under each foot rooting like a tripod, weight evenly spread.</li><li>Knees not locked, thighs slightly rotating in, tailbone naturally down.</li><li>The crown reaches up, chin parallel to the floor.</li><li>Once grounded, bring attention back to the breath — the stillness before the flow.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/arms-up.jpg" alt="Arms up" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>18</small>Arms up</div><div class="t">s1 15:35</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>The first inhale of the sun salutation.</b></p><p>From mountain pose, inhale, sweep the arms up overhead; the whole body lengthens from the soles to the fingertips.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Feet keep rooting — the higher the arms go, the more the feet press down.</li><li>Don't hunch: shoulder blades slide down, arms beside the ears.</li><li>Ribs don't flare: draw the lower ribs in slightly instead of arching back to reach higher.</li><li>Make this inhale full and slow, preparing the forward fold.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/standing-fold.jpg" alt="Standing forward fold" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>19</small>Standing forward fold</div><div class="t">s1 15:48</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Uttanasana: fold from the hips.</b></p><p>Exhale, fold forward from the hips, hands beside the feet, knees soft. Inhale, press hands into the floor or shins, lift the head and lengthen the front body.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>The hinge is in the hips, not the waist — the belly comes toward the thighs first.</li><li>Bend the knees as much as you need for the hands to reach the floor without rounding the back.</li><li>On the half lift the spine becomes a flat back, ready to step back.</li><li>Weight toward the balls of the feet, not all in the heels.</li></ul></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/plank.jpg" alt="Plank" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>20</small>Plank</div><div class="t">s1 15:54</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Plank: lengthen yourself from both ends.</b></p><p>Exhale, step the feet back one after the other into plank. Feet hip-width, shoulders over the wrists.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Tailbone lengthens toward the heels while the pubic bone draws gently toward the head — like pulling a drawer open from both ends.</li><li>Belly slightly engaged; one straight line from head to heels — no sagging, no hips up.</li><li>Fingers spread and grip the floor; push away so the upper back stays full, shoulder blades don't collapse.</li><li>The neck continues the spine; gaze just in front of the hands.</li></ul><p>Easier: kneeling plank — knees down, same straight line.</p></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/chaturanga.jpg" alt="Chaturanga" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>21</small>Chaturanga</div><div class="t">s1 15:58</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Shoulders past the wrists first, elbows close as you lower.</b></p><p>Inhale, shift the weight forward until the shoulders are past the wrists. Exhale, elbows close to the body, lower the upper body as one unit about halfway down, elbows around 90 degrees.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>"Forward first, then down" — without shifting forward the elbows can't reach 90° and the load goes into the front of the shoulders.</li><li>Elbows hug the body and point back, not out.</li><li>The whole body lowers like one plank — not chest first, not hips first.</li><li>Feel the chest opening; the chin may move slightly forward.</li><li>Stop at the height you can control — no need to reach the floor.</li></ul><p>Easier: knees down — first the knees, then lower the upper body as one unit, elbows close, about 90°. Practise with 80 % effort; the easier version is practice too.</p></details></div></div></div>
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/updog.jpg" alt="Upward-facing dog" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>22</small>Upward-facing dog</div><div class="t">s1 16:02</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Press the tops of the feet, chest forward and up.</b></p><p>Inhale, press the tops of the feet into the mat, push the hands down, lift the upper body, chest opens forward and up — upward-facing dog. Stretch the whole front body.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>The tops of the feet and the hands are the only support; thighs and knees lift off the floor if possible.</li><li>Hands press and fingers grip; collarbones widen, shoulder blades slide down the back.</li><li>Shoulders away from the ears — if they hunch, the hands are too far forward or not pressing enough.</li><li>The backbend comes from opening the upper back; the belly draws in slightly and the pubic bone moves forward to protect the lower back.</li></ul><p>Easier: cobra, thighs stay on the floor.</p></details></div></div></div>

## 4 · Standing sequence

<p class="note">Warriors, triangle, pyramid, crescent, dragon, splits, pigeon — one full side.</p>
<div class="flow-grid">
<div class="flow-card"><div class="m"><img src="figures/video/warrior-i.jpg" alt="Warrior I" loading="lazy"><span class="tag">still</span></div><div class="b"><div class="n"><small>30</small>Warrior I</div><div class="t">s1 17:15</div><div class="s"><details class="ex"><summary>Guide</summary><p><b>Reach up, root down.</b></p><p>Inhale, lift the leg; exhale, step the foot between the hands. When stable, slowly sink until the thigh is parallel to the floor.</p><p>Key points:</p><ul><li>Front knee over the ankle, pointing toward the second and third toes.</li><li>Pelvis faces forward as much as possible; draw the hip of the back leg forward.</li><li>Inhale — feel the body grow up from the midline; exhale — root deeper into the feet.</li><li>Image: a small boat on a lake following the waves — not complete stillness, but finding stability within the movement.</li></ul><p>Easier: back knee down, low lunge.</p></details></div></div></div>

## 5 · Balance challenge

<p class="note">Starting from chair: airplane, knee hug, standing hand to toe, figure-four balance.</p>
<div class="flow-grid">

## 6 · Seated stretches

<p class="note">Head-to-knee, Marichyasana, half lotus, compass, finishing with reverse tabletop and wild thing.</p>

## 7 · Relaxation & inversions

<p class="note">Reclined bound angle and savasana; then entering and leaving wheel, plow, shoulder stand and headstand.</p>
