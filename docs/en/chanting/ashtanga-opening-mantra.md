# Ashtanga Opening Mantra

<p class="pose-sanskrit">阿斯汤加开课唱诵 · Vande Gurūṇāṃ</p>

In the Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga tradition this invocation is chanted before each practice. The first half honours the lineage of **teachers** (guru), the second half honours the sage **Patañjali**, who compiled the Yoga Sūtras.
The point is not religious: we begin with gratitude and humility, and let the mind settle first.

## Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

<div class="mantra"><div class="dev">ॐ<br>
वन्दे गुरूणां चरणारविन्दे<br>
सन्दर्शित स्वात्म सुखाव बोधे ।<br>
निःश्रेयसे जाङ्गलिकायमाने<br>
संसार हालाहल मोहशान्त्यै ॥<br><br>
आबाहु पुरुषाकारं<br>
शङ्खचक्रासि धारिणम् ।<br>
सहस्र शिरसं श्वेतं<br>
प्रणमामि पतञ्जलिम् ॥<br>
ॐ</div></div>

## Line by line

| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *vande gurūṇāṁ caraṇāravinde* | I bow to the lotus feet of the teachers, |
| *sandarśita svātma sukhāva bodhe* | which reveal and awaken the joy of our true Self; |
| *niḥ-śreyase jāṅgalikāyamāne* | they are the highest refuge, like a healer in the jungle, |
| *saṁsāra hālāhala mohaśāntyai* | calming the delusion caused by the poison of conditioned existence. |
| *ābāhu puruṣākāraṁ* | Human in form up to the arms, |
| *śaṅkha-cakrāsi dhāriṇam* | holding a conch, a discus and a sword, |
| *sahasra śirasaṁ śvetaṁ* | with a thousand radiant white heads (as the serpent Ananta) — |
| *praṇamāmi patañjalim* | to Patañjali I bow. |

**Symbols**: conch = sacred sound (OM) · discus = light and infinite time · sword = discriminating wisdom · thousand-headed serpent = infinity and awareness.

## Pronunciation tips

- A line over a vowel (ā ī ū) = long vowel, hold it a little.
- **ś / ṣ** are close to "sh"; **c** is "ch" (caraṇa = cha-ra-na).
- **ṁ** is a nasal ending, a soft "mm".
- **ñ** is a nasal "ny" (patañjali ≈ pa-tan-ja-li).

<div class="mantra-tip">
<b>Teaching tips</b><br>
· Sit tall, palms together at the heart (Añjali Mudrā), eyes may close.<br>
· Traditionally the teacher chants a line and students repeat; you can also play the video a few times first.<br>
· Chant one OM before and after.<br>
· Tell students they may simply listen and breathe — chanting is an invitation, not a requirement.
